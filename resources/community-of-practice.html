join our Community of Practice (CoP) for Climate Education Practitioners

Join our brand new Community of Practice! Each session is designed to convene climate education practitioners across Metro Vancouver in a flexible and broad sense- whether you’re a traditional classroom teacher, a youth-serving nonprofit worker, a curriculum consultant, a resource creator or anyone else that educates young people on all things sustainability, climate, social impact, and more.

We believe that peer learning, resource-sharing, and active collaboration bolsters the ecosystem and strengthens our shared causes.

Our kick-off meeting will be spent:
  • Co-envisioning of the future of our CoP - what communities or organizations do you represent, and what would you like to contribute and gain from this convening? Are there any sessions you’ve been yearning to lead and need an audience for? Any ideas or projects in the works you’d love to bounce off of like-missioned folks?
  • Sharing our newly published report on NGO-classroom partnerships for sustainability education in Metro Vancouver. The report, “Teachers Know Best,” summarizes findings from a focus group held with secondary school teachers who shared their successes, challenges, and needs regarding supplemental and co-curricular NGO-led sustainability programming!

If you are a climate education practitioner in Metro Vancouver, we look forward to designing, problem-solving, networking, and building with you!

Membership to the CoP for Climate Education Practitioners is free to join. To register to join the community, visit this link.

Some key questions we aim to explore as a collective:

How do we navigate the tension between urgency and capacity? Given climate crisis timelines and limited educator bandwidth, how do we prioritize impact without burning out practitioners?

How do we address the "green ceiling" in climate education? What structures prevent equity-deserving youth from accessing climate career pathways, and how can educators actively dismantle these barriers?

How can we avoid duplication and fill actual gaps? What climate education needs are over-served vs. under-served in Metro Vancouver, and how do we coordinate accordingly?

What does effective resource-sharing infrastructure look like? Should we create a shared curriculum library, guest speaker database, funding opportunity tracker, or workshop template repository?

frequently asked questions:

is there a cost to join?

no, the CoP for Climate Education Practitioners is completely free to join and participate in.

how often does the CoP for Climate Education Practitioners meet?

we're co-designing the meeting schedule together at our kick-off session based on participant availability and interest. expect sessions to be monthly or once every 2-3 months, with flexibility for workshop-style deep dives or shorter gathering sessions as needed.

temperature check: how do you feel about the climate?

climate anxiety

FUTURE-FACING

fear, stress, or worry stemming from a sense of impending climate change impacts and processes; a sense of threat & uncertainty about environmental doom. can be mental, emotional, physical

eco-grief

MOURNING

grief from experienced or anticipated ecological losses, including the loss of species, ecosystems and meaningful landscapes due to acute or chronic environmental change

solastalgia

IN-THE-PROCESS

distress that is produced by negatively perceived environmental change impacting on people while they are directly connected to their home environment

climate hope

PERSEVERING

the acknowledgement of the climate crisis, and the simultaneous focus on the power of collective action to create a sustainable future; the unyielding investment in individual and community agency to make a difference

Did you know? In a research project surveying 1,000 young people aged 16-25 years in Canada, 78% of youth expressed that climate change affects their overall mental health. 37% reported that their feelings about climate change negatively impacted daily functioning.
